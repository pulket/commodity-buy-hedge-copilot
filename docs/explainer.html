1. What's different from a simple rule

Instead of a rule of thumb, the engine simulates thousands of possible price futures and picks the buy/hedge mix that is best on a metric combining cost AND stability. It runs this for every commodity, then nets the risks across the portfolio. Four stages:

S
Sense - read the market for each commodity: 6 signals from price, curve, FX & freight.
S
Score - turn signals into a 0-100 Buy-Now score (buy / phase / wait).
D
Decide - Monte-Carlo the cost of every candidate strategy and pick the one that minimises RALC (below), inside the appetite caps.
A
Act - auto-execute if the order sits inside the council's guardrails, otherwise escalate.

3. Supply chain — from demand to how much we ACTUALLY buy

Price is only half the story. Before pricing anything, the engine turns demand, inventory, lead time and supplier limits into the real volume to source — and the non-negotiable "must-buy-now" quantity that protects supply continuity. Demand and its volatility are read from the company's real sales file (~114,950 units/yr, demand swings CV 21% month-to-month) — not assumed. For Palm oil (CPO):

D

Decide - the RALC metric and the efficient frontier

You asked for a metric that balances cost and stability. The engine minimises:

RALC = E[Cost] + λ × SD[Cost]
expected cost + a penalty on how much that cost can swing. λ (risk-aversion) = 0.8 for the "balanced" preset (Conservative 1.5 · Balanced 0.8 · Aggressive 0.3).

Each dot below is one candidate strategy (a cover% × hedge% mix), scored by Monte-Carlo on expected cost (left = cheaper) vs risk (down = steadier). The purple line is the efficient frontier - the best you can do. The engine picks the frontier point that matches the appetite's λ.

The chosen plan — and why it is 100%, not 110%. The engine picks cover = 60% and hedge = 50%. But "hedge 50%" means 50% of the un-locked 40%, not of the total. So the real split of the net requirement is:
  • 60% locked now via physical forwards (= cover 60%)
  • 20% option-hedged (= 50% × the remaining 40%)
  • 20% left floating (the rest of the remaining 40%)
60 + 20 + 20 = 100%.

Why 60% & 50% exactly? Both hit the "balanced" preset's caps — the optimiser wanted to reduce risk further, but the caps stop it: cover is limited to 60% (the smaller of the appetite cap 60% and what suppliers will commit, 80%) and hedge to 50%. Result: E[cost] ₹679 Cr, risk ₹16.0 Cr, CaR95 ₹703 Cr. Instrument: Physical forwards (cover) + call options/caps (hedge).

The pay-off distribution (why it's insurance)

Running 20,000 price futures gives the full range of possible total cost. Do-nothing (red) has a long expensive tail; the engine plan (purple) is far tighter - a much lower worst case (CaR95).

Do-nothing worst-case (95%) ₹755 Cr vs engine ₹703 Cr - the plan trims the bad tail. Expected cost is about the same (₹668 vs ₹679 Cr): you buy stability, not a discount.

WHEN to buy - physical (with holding cost) vs hedge

Cover% and hedge% answer how much; this answers when. A dynamic program schedules buys week-by-week on the forward price path, charging a holding cost of 1.25%/month (finance + storage + insurance) for anything bought early, and respecting the 6-week lead time. Each plan is then priced over the 20,000 paths (so it carries real price risk) and ranked on RALC. The core trade-off is simple: is the forward price you would lock cheaper than buying at spot and paying to hold it?

7. Where exactly the AI / LLM is used (and where it is NOT)

This is important: the AI does not invent the numbers. The pricing maths (RALC, Monte-Carlo, the frontier) stays a transparent, auditable engine. The AI sits around it — it turns messy real-world text into structured, cited inputs, explains and drafts, and executes inside hard limits. Here is every touchpoint, mapped to the four stages:

StageAI componentTechniqueReadsProducesHuman control

DECIDEScenario forecastingML (+LLM)Price/vol history + the LLM's event readProbabilities for the price paths (replaces the plain random walk); a fatter up-tail if an export ban is likelyCouncil sets the risk preset (λ, caps)
DECIDEAnomaly detectionMLLive price, FX, freight, basisFlags an odd move and auto-fires the matching triggerAlerts a human; thresholds are set
ACTAgentic execution & draftingLLM agentThe engine's chosen plan + the policy capsDrafts the order, checks it vs guardrails, auto-executes inside caps or writes the escalation memoHard caps + manual override; every action audited
ALLNatural-language Q&A & explanationsLLMA plain-English question + the live model"Margin if palm +10% & rupee −3%?" → runs the model & explains; also writes the council memo & weekly briefAnswers link back to the model run

The one rule that makes it trustworthy: the LLM converts text → structured, cited input and drafts/explains/executes; it never sets a price or overrides a guardrail. So you get AI's reach over messy real-world information plus a fully auditable number engine — exactly what a large organisation can pilot.
